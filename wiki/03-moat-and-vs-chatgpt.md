# 03 — Moat & Why Not Just Use ChatGPT or Claude?

Bob asked the most important question for us to answer honestly:
> *"What is the delta between our app and someone just opening ChatGPT or Claude Code out of the box? And what is our moat?"*

 *(Note: We should **not** lean on the claim that modern LLMs are too sycophantic to tell when someone is wrong—modern frontier models can recognize wrong answers when prompted. Our real advantage comes from taste, structure, interface, reuse, calibration, and memory over time.)*

---

## The 6 Reasons Our Platform Beats Raw ChatGPT/Claude (and Compounds Into a Moat)

### 1. LLMs have knowledge, but they don't have taste in how to teach—our platform captures human taste
- If you open Claude or ChatGPT and type *"Grill me on how Transformers work,"* the model has to respond to you and make up questions on the fly in a single turn. Because it has no taste for what actually matters versus textbook trivia, half of its questions feel flat, repetitive, or overly pedantic—and when it tries to invent a trick question on the spot, the trick is often ambiguous.
- Just as there are better and worse human teachers, there are better and worse Kenjitsu lessons.
- On our platform, lessons are **saved, reused, and shaped by humans** in two ways:
  1. **Creators and domain experts supply taste upfront:** Because AI makes drafting a lesson cheap, someone who deeply understands *The Great Leap Forward* or *Distributed Caching* doesn't have to spend 40 hours filming videos. They can spend 5 minutes steering the AI, editing a weak round, or forking an existing fight to focus on the exact scenarios and misconceptions that matter.
  2. **Learners filter out weak questions over time:** In ChatGPT, a conversation disappears when you close the tab. On our platform, when hundreds of people play the same fight, human ratings, completion rates, and error patterns show which questions actually work and which ones are dull or confusing, so the best fights rise to the top of the shelf.

### 2. Most people won't think to ask a chatbot to teach them via questions—and even if they do, a chat box gives you no map or finish line
- For every person who knows how to prompt Claude to teach them Socratically, there are ten people who would never think to do it.
- More importantly, when you ask a chatbot to quiz you, you have no sense of where you are or when you're done. Four turns in, you don't know how much of the subject you've covered, what blind spots remain, or what to learn next.
- In our app, knowledge is organized into a visible **Focus Map** and a **zoomable 3D Tree of Knowledge** where nodes light up as you pass them and earn belts—giving you a clear sense of progress and a map of what to explore next.

### 3. An interface built for active learning beats typing essays into a chat box
- Typing long paragraphs into a chat window gets tiring fast.
- Our interface can show a 4-step argument or code snippet and let you click the exact step where the logic breaks, highlight buzzwords in your answer when you haven't explained what they mean, track your thinking time separately from reading time, and give you a 1-click button to break a hard question down into smaller stepping stones when you get stuck.

### 4. Reusing fights makes them instant, cheap, and verifiable
- In ChatGPT, every user pays the latency and token cost of generating everything from scratch.
- On our platform, once a fight on a public topic is created and vetted, Rounds 1–4 are cached and reused across thousands of learners—so questions load in 0ms and cost us half as much to run.

### 5. We show you how you rank against other people
- At the end of a 15-minute chat with Claude, you have no idea if your answers were actually strong compared to other people preparing for the same interview or learning the same subject.
- Because thousands of users run the exact same fights on our platform, we can give you a real benchmark (for example, showing you privately that you finished in the 91st percentile in 2m 15s).

### 6. We track what you know over time and use spaced repetition so you don't forget
- ChatGPT is stateless across your learning life—it doesn't remember which specific concept tripped you up two weeks ago and tap you on the shoulder to test you again.
- Like Anki, our platform owns your long-term mastery record across every node you've fought, scheduling quick spaced rematches on the concepts you nearly missed so your belts don't decay.
